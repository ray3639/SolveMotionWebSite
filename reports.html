Report Output Model

Reports are designed to translate Core-owned intelligence into reviewable explanations. The goal is not to hide reasoning behind a score, but to show what the system saw, what it did not know, and why the next action was recommended.

Confidence

Shows how strongly Core 12.0 can support an interpretation from available evidence.

Risk

Frames caution, safety context, contradiction, uncertainty, or review need.

Recommendation

Explains the governed next action and why that action is appropriate.

Missing Evidence

Identifies what is not known so the report does not overstate certainty.

Trajectory

Shows longer-term direction across sessions, learning, cases, readiness, or recovery.

Trend

Summarizes current movement across repeated observations or changing context.

Professional Review Layer

Reports are written for review, explanation, and structured decision support. They are not substitutes for clinical authority, legal authority, pharmacy policy, emergency care, coaching judgment, or professional responsibility.

  • Reports explain outputs instead of hiding them behind unexplained scores.
  • Reports show missing evidence so users understand uncertainty.
  • Reports preserve human-governed boundaries and decision-support framing.
  • Reports help compare sessions, patterns, cases, learning progress, and domain context.

Enterprise Report Use

Enterprise use requires auditability, release discipline, repeatable outputs, and clear governance. Reports provide a review-ready surface for pilots, demos, stakeholder review, and implementation planning.

Auditability

Reports make Core output easier to review across users, sessions, and product domains.

Release Gates

Report language can be checked against doctrine, baselines, and no-overstatement rules.

Governance

Reports preserve the boundary between Core-owned reasoning and render-only applications.

Baselines

Report structure can be versioned alongside product and platform release checkpoints.

Report Boundaries

Reports are explanatory and review-supportive. They do not diagnose, prescribe, determine legal conclusions, replace professional review, or guarantee outcomes. They should always preserve the difference between completed Core-connected products and in-progress product direction.
